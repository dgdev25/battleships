why the next shot is locked

Before you fire, the AI has already chosen its own target and shown you a fingerprint of it. After the round this page reopens the move, rebuilds the fingerprint and checks the two match — a single changed letter would scramble it. That proves the target did not change after you fired. It does not prove the target was chosen fairly: the opponent and the checker both run in this one process, so treat it as a working commit-and-reveal demonstration, not a referee.

what the doctrines change

These change what the AI does with a read, not how hard it reads you — the prediction runs at full strength in all three. Dominate fires at the cell its memory rates highest. Level steers toward a tied score, wasting shots when it is ahead. Yield waits until its read is strong, then deliberately fires at the water it is most sure is empty; on a weak read it holds mid-table rather than throwing the shot at random.

how to read these numbers

Read rate is how often the AI named your exact next cell before you fired it. The number to beat is 1%, not 50% — a blind guess on a hundred-cell grid is right once in a hundred. The sub-line also gives its within-two-cells rate, where blind guessing sits near 13%. Read rate is not the score: in Level or Yield the AI throws shots away on purpose, so you can be far ahead on points while it still reads you perfectly.

how it reads you

After every game the AI writes one short note per square of your ocean — where the square sits, how close to an edge or a corner, which parity, which band — and stores it against the truth of whether a hull was there. Those notes become fixed lists of 64 numbers in a ruvector store, so squares that resemble each other land near each other.

Before each of its shots it writes the note for every square it has not tried yet, finds the closest notes in memory, and lets them vote on whether you hide ships in places like that. Closer and more recent memories count for more. It is not reasoning about naval tactics — it is asking “when a square looked like this before, was there a hull under it?” A fresh memory gets classic hunt-and-target play; the more games you leave behind, the more the opening shots stop looking like guesses.

Each dot is one recalled memory: nearer the centre means more similar, bigger means it carried more weight, red means that memory held a hull.
